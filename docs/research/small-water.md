# Small water pilot

JRC Global Surface Water v1.4 maps Landsat observations through 2021 at 30 m. Its occurrence band summarizes the frequency of observed water across the record; seasonality describes the number of months water is present. It is historical evidence, not a current water forecast. Ponds smaller than a pixel, water under vegetation, and periods without clear observations may be missed. A water pixel alone does not establish access.

The source is available free under the Copernicus data policy with required acknowledgement and citation: [JRC dataset and terms](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/JRC_GSW1_4_GlobalSurfaceWater), Pekel et al., *Nature* 540, 418–422 (2016), doi:10.1038/nature20584. Source metadata reviewed 2026-10-26.

`scripts/small-water/jrc-pilot.py` exports occurrence and seasonality for a 0.2° square in Wisconsin. Earth Engine task `JMD2XJ23E35HRUYMAK6ALFC3` used 0.709 EECU-seconds (0.00020 EECU-hours). This small export is not enough to estimate national cost or establish accuracy. It unmask-fills missing values with zero for exploration; the source masks areas where water was never detected, and its occurrence band has a fractional mask. A production encoding must account for masks and observation coverage rather than treating zero as proof of absence.

A Wisconsin state-sized export (`JSXDC53Y7C2GVECQ4KMQGRV4`) was cancelled after it exceeded its planned 1 EECU-hour estimate; final usage was 1.08997 EECU-hours. A simple national area extrapolation from the partial state cost already exceeds the 40 EECU-hour layer budget, though scaling is only approximate. Do not use this export recipe nationally without a cheaper measured approach. Before publishing, choose a representation that preserves missing-data semantics, evaluate whether it adds useful information to existing NHD and wetlands layers in several environments, and measure storage and national processing costs. No layer has been published from this pilot.

## Coverage assessment (2026-10-26)

The shipped Surface water history layer already displays the JRC occurrence footprint for 1984–2024. A small-water layer derived only by thresholding or selecting connected patches from the same occurrence data could highlight a subset, but cannot discover water outside that footprint. The pilot's older v1.4 occurrence ends in 2021, so publishing it would also lose three years of observations relative to the existing layer. JRC's seasonality band adds timing information, but does not establish a new small-pond footprint; v1.4 seasonality and v1.5 recent seasonality cover different periods. Neither supplies current conditions. See [JRC data access and version notes](https://global-surface-water.appspot.com/download) and the [v1.4 catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/JRC_GSW1_4_GlobalSurfaceWater).

NHD maps hydrographic features, while NWI maps wetlands rather than just open water. These are different products from JRC, and [USFWS documents omissions and limitations in NWI](https://www.fws.gov/page/wetlands-data-limitations-exclusions-and-precautions). This comparison does not quantify overlap or establish that any particular pond is missing from either inventory. Landsat's 30 m resolution and vegetation cover limit JRC's ability to detect the smallest or covered ponds. A genuinely complementary product would require a different source or method and representative independent comparisons, including false positives and seasonal dates, before national processing. No such product is justified by the current pilot; do not run the existing JRC recipe nationally.

The Wisconsin export was cancelled at 3,923.881 EECU-seconds (1.08997 EECU-hours); its partial cost remains unsuitable as a precise national estimate.


## Alternative source shortlist (2026-10-26)

These are candidates for a comparison, not validated replacements or published layers. None establishes public access or current water conditions.

- [Dynamic World V1](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_DYNAMICWORLD_V1) supplies 10 m Sentinel-2-based per-image water and flooded-vegetation probabilities from 2015 onward. Its finer grid could resolve some smaller features than Landsat, but a maximum over many dates can accumulate false positives, and cloud/shadow masks and observation counts matter. The catalog cautions about bright arid surfaces and sunglint; use confidence thresholds and retain observation coverage. It is CC BY 4.0 with the catalog's required attribution. A national composite still needs a measured Earth Engine cost and storage estimate.
- [USGS DSWE annual proportions](https://www.usgs.gov/data/collection-2-dynamic-surface-water-extent-dswe-proportions-conus-annual-proportions-1984) provides CONUS 30 m annual GeoTIFFs with open-water, partial-water and valid-observation bands, and is CC0. Its partial-water classification may offer information beyond JRC open-water occurrence, but both use Landsat and the same 30 m grid scale; finer resampling does not add measured detail. Annual proportions do not identify the date of an observation. Distribution, storage cost, and usefulness alongside existing layers remain unverified.
- [NASA OPERA DSWx](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2024-04/OPERA-DSWx_Fact_Sheet.pdf) provides 30 m observation-based optical and radar products; radar may offer different evidence for inundated vegetation. Availability, processing cost, permissions and the benefit over existing maps need evaluation before use.

For the next comparison, select independent, date-matched high-resolution reference areas across distinct wetland and dryland settings, including known small ponds and non-water lookalikes. Compare candidate detections with reference imagery and the existing NHD, NWI and JRC footprints, recording omissions, false positives and observation dates rather than assuming a mismatch is an error. Evaluate thresholds on separate areas from those used to choose them. No new export or R2 upload was performed for this shortlist.
